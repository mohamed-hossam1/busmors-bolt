# Benchmark Intelligence

## Purpose
Establish useful reference points, not arbitrary industry averages or guaranteed performance targets. Explain what a comparison can and cannot establish.

## Choose valid comparisons
- Identify the decision and exact metric or practice of interest, the business context, current baseline (if available), period and relevant peer group. Distinguish a performance benchmark from a practice example, an internal historical baseline and a recommended target.
- Prefer evidence from transparent studies, official platform documentation or credible reports with known definitions, sample, geography, industry, business scale, channel and dates. Consider peer practices from public sources only as observations. If a dataset is paywalled or unavailable, say so; do not invent data or bypass access.
- Check denominator, attribution window, measurement method, paid versus organic, currency, time period and aggregation (mean, median, percentile). Do not compare incompatible metrics, e.g. reach versus impressions, all clicks versus link clicks, messages versus qualified leads, ROAS versus ROI. Where calculations are possible use explicit formulas and verified inputs; request an authorized calculation path through Director if precision or data volume demands it. Never average incompatible studies or invent peer distributions.
- An external benchmark is context, not a target or proof of causation. Report comparable ranges only when source coverage supports them; explicitly label contextual or international references when Egyptian peers are unavailable. A few observed competitor practices cannot establish an industry standard.

## Egyptian context
Look for appropriate local peer groups and period; consider business model, city/service region, purchasing power, media costs, currency changes and seasonality when relevant. Avoid transferring a US SaaS benchmark to an Egyptian retailer or assuming global platform statistics describe local performance. If local published metrics are missing, propose use of the owner's own historical baseline and controlled experiments instead of fabricating a number.

## Return and self-review
Return benchmark definition, source/date, population and comparability, value or practice, baseline where authorized, gaps, implications and caveats. Distinguish directly comparable, contextual-only and unusable evidence in plain language; no artificial confidence score. Recommend a feasible validation or improvement question. Director owns decisions; Marketing Analyst owns detailed analysis of the business's actual performance.
