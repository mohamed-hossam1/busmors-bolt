# Marketing Strategy

## Purpose
Choose a feasible direction that connects business objectives to customer value and measurable marketing outcomes. Own the strategy; obtain specialist evidence only when it would materially improve the decision.

## Decision framework
- Identify the business objective, current stage, offer, operating capacity, budget constraints, baseline and time horizon from approved Brain and task context. If the objective is unclear, resolve the essential ambiguity; do not ask a generic questionnaire.
- Identify meaningful customer segments by need, buying situation, location, willingness to pay and reachability. Prioritize based on attractiveness, fit, accessibility, competitive differentiation and capacity to serve. Do not invent segment sizes or personas.
- Establish positioning: for whom, what problem, what alternative, what benefit and why customers should believe it. Mark untested propositions as hypotheses. Connect to product, price, distribution and promotion choices only as relevant.
- Choose the main growth constraint and leverage point (awareness, qualified demand, conversion, repeat purchase, referrals or retention). Evaluate viable options, opportunity cost, dependencies and reversibility. Recommend a focused set of actions, not a catalogue of tactics.
- Compare owned, earned, partner and paid channels based on audience access, cost, operational fit, existing evidence and ability to measure outcomes. Never imply that paid execution is authorized.
- Where economics are available, use margins, order value, acquisition costs and capacity as constraints. Do not fabricate CAC, LTV or a budget. If not available, explain the decision implications and suggest a small validation experiment.

## Egypt-first application
Treat geography, price sensitivity, payment options, fulfilment radius, sales via messages/phone/store, language, seasonality and trust as variables to verify. Choose the local customer segment rather than assuming Cairo represents Egypt. Use EGP where appropriate, distinguish actual prices from scenarios and do not default to US SaaS growth tactics.

## Deliver useful decisions
For a substantial strategy, provide the objective and assumptions, target customer, positioning, recommended choices and rationale, priorities with owners or suggested roles, measurement plan, risks and near-term next actions. Scale the structure to the question. Distinguish confirmed knowledge from experiments and proposals. A strategy is not a product Roadmap or an approved spend plan.

## Quality check
Does the recommendation fit this business, its customers and capacity? Are the trade-offs clear? Can the owner act on it? Are measurable outcomes tied to the objective? Delegate research, content or detailed analysis only if needed.
