# Strategic Problem Solving

## Purpose
Turn an unclear marketing problem into a defensible decision rather than guessing a cause or prescribing generic tactics.

## Diagnose before prescribing
- Restate the decision and observable symptom: what changed, for whom, where, over what period, relative to what expectation or baseline? Distinguish symptoms from causes. Establish the business outcome that matters.
- Locate the bottleneck across demand, message/offer, audience, conversion path, fulfilment, follow-up, retention or measurement. Use a causal map, repeated why-questions or a simple funnel breakdown only when useful. Do not force a fixed number of whys or assume one root cause.
- Form a small set of plausible competing explanations. For each, identify what supports it, what contradicts it and what observation could distinguish it from the alternatives. Separate correlation from causation. Check data quality, changes in spend/traffic mix, seasonality, stock, response times and other plausible confounders as relevant.
- Use current Brain, prior reports and available metrics first. Ask only for information whose absence prevents meaningful work; otherwise provide conditional options. Delegate extensive evidence gathering to Research Agent and detailed quantitative diagnosis to Marketing Analyst.

## Choose and validate
Compare interventions on expected benefit, evidence, effort, cost, risk, dependency and reversibility. Avoid fabricated numerical scores. Prefer a low-risk, informative experiment when uncertainty is high. For a consequential choice, state the best-supported alternative and why others are not preferred. Identify the success metric, baseline, observation window, stopping/review condition and who should act. Avoid claiming statistical certainty without adequate evidence. A recommendation to test does not authorize spend or changes in live systems.

## Deliver
Explain the problem, leading hypotheses, evidence and gaps, recommended actions in priority order and a practical validation step. Scale detail to the decision. In Egypt, account for EGP costs, local lead handling, stock/delivery availability, seasonality and message-to-order conversion where relevant. If no credible diagnosis is possible, state what data is needed instead of inventing a cause.

## Quality check
Would the evidence distinguish the chosen explanation from alternatives? Is the action feasible, reversible where possible, and tied to the business outcome? Are assumptions explicit?
