# Comparative Analysis

## Method
- Define the decision and comparison units. Record source, currency, timezone, date boundaries, observation grain, attribution basis, conversion definition and eligible population for each group. Compare complete periods or explicitly matched partial windows, and distinguish rolling from calendar intervals.
- Check whether comparison is meaningful: similar objective, maturity, audience, delivery mix, measurement method and exposure. For posts use comparable age when possible; for conversions allow appropriate observation time. Align data to the coarsest reliable grain and avoid joining totals at mismatched levels.
- Calculate absolute change, relative change and percentage-point difference where appropriate. Show denominators and sample counts. A change from zero cannot have an ordinary relative growth rate. Weighted overall rates require compatible counts. Do not sum unique exposure over overlapping slices. Inspect whether shifts in segment mix explain the aggregate trend; show both the aggregate and relevant segment results if supported.
- External benchmarks must come from an approved Director-supplied source or prior Deliverable, with date, geography, sector, channel, metric definition and method. Do not compare mismatched definitions as if they were equal. Use an internal historical baseline when a suitable external benchmark is unavailable; state that the external comparison is unknown.

## Egyptian relevance
Account for actual local operating days, campaign dates, seasonal events, price changes and service-area differences when relevant. Compare EGP figures on the same stated basis; if currency conversion or inflation adjustment is essential, request a verified method through Director rather than guessing. For cash-on-delivery or lead-based businesses use the same order or lead stage in both periods.

## Return and review
Provide a comparison table with source and period, values, bases, changes, qualification notes and implications. Identify which differences are directly observed and which may be confounded. Avoid declaring a statistically significant winner from aggregate counts unless a suitable validated method and data support that conclusion. Flag small samples, unmatched groups and missing baselines. Recommend a decision or the next data needed. Never collect external benchmarks directly or modify any marketing asset.
