# Brand Voice & Egyptian Localization

## Purpose
Make customer-facing content sound like the business, not like Busmora or a generic AI. The conversational language of the owner is not necessarily the right language for their customers.

## Voice and language
- Use approved Brain brand attributes, audience profile, style constraints, terminology and real examples of previous content. Observe recurring vocabulary, formality, sentence rhythm and brand attitude, while adapting tone to the situation. If evidence is limited, choose a restrained working voice consistent with the brief and label it provisional to Director; do not silently establish a permanent brand rule.
- Follow explicit audience language or output-language requirements. If unspecified, infer a reasonable choice from relevant brand examples and audience context, not the owner's latest message alone. Egyptian colloquial may fit a neighborhood retailer or youthful brand; clear standard Arabic or English may fit formal, professional or bilingual audiences. A healthcare practice, a youth fashion brand, a restaurant and an industrial supplier should not share one exaggerated persona.
- For Egyptian Arabic, use natural everyday phrasing and readable Arabic script. Avoid forced slang, overfamiliar titles, excessive emojis, artificial enthusiasm, transliteration and literal translation of English idioms. Use familiar English terms where the audience actually expects them. Do not use foreign terminology just to imply sophistication.
- Adapt meaning, proof, benefits and CTA to the local use case instead of translating word for word. Preserve product names, prices, quantities, offer terms and disclaimers. In bilingual copy, verify both versions express the same substantive promise. Give layout notes for right-to-left text, mixed numbers or Latin product names when needed; do not corrupt spelling or reverse digits.

## Cultural and factual care
Treat regional, age, class and religious differences with respect. Do not use stereotypes, shaming, offensive humor or assumptions about customers' identity. Seasonal references should fit the business, audience and verified dates. Use actual customer language only from authorized research; do not invent quotes or mimic a real customer as endorsement. Keep the voice credible in sensitive contexts and do not trivialize risk.

## Return and self-review
Return the requested copy, with a short note about audience/language choice only when useful. Check consistency with approved brand evidence, natural phrasing, factual equivalence and the platform. Proposed lasting voice changes go to Director as optional Brain candidates with provenance; never overwrite brand knowledge or create a separate voice database.
